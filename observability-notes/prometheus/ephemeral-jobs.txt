While learning more about Prometheus monitoring, I came across an interesting challenge: **How do you monitor ephemeral jobs?**

Prometheus follows a **pull-based model** — it periodically reaches out to a target and scrapes its `/metrics` endpoint.

That works well for long-running applications.

But consider a Kubernetes Job that runs for only **5 seconds**, while Prometheus scrapes every **15 seconds**.

The timeline could look like this:

Job starts → runs for 5s → completes ❌
Prometheus scrape happens at 15s → the pod is already gone.

So the problem isn't that Prometheus *can't* scrape ephemeral workloads.

The problem is that **the workload must still be alive and discoverable when the scrape happens**.

For longer-running jobs, normal Prometheus scraping with Kubernetes service discovery can work perfectly well.

For very short-lived batch jobs, we may need a different approach, such as **Prometheus Pushgateway**, where the job pushes its metrics before terminating and Prometheus later scrapes those metrics from the Pushgateway.

A simple concept, but an important distinction:

**Pull-based monitoring works only when the target is available when the pull happens.**
